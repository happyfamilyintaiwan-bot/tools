Shared dishes are split evenly, and the service charge and discounts are shared in proportion to what each person ordered. You can also let the birthday person eat for free. Share the result as a link or text so everyone can pay back whoever covered the bill.

How to split a restaurant bill in four steps

  1. Add everyone at the table: type the names, or paste several at once separated by commas. Remember to add yourself.
  2. Add what was ordered: copy the receipt text with your phone's built-in text recognition and paste it in, and the items are created for you. You can also type them by hand. Then tap who ordered each item. Pick several people for shared dishes and the cost is split between them. Only know the total? Switch to “Split evenly”.
  3. Set the service charge and who paid: choose the service charge rate, enter any discount, mark anyone who is being treated (a birthday, for example), and pick under “Who paid the bill?” who paid at the counter.
  4. Share the result: the results panel shows what each person owes and who to pay. Tap “Copy summary” and paste it into your group chat.

Why the service charge is split by what you ordered

Each person pays their own items + their part of shared items + a share of (service charge − discount) in proportion to what they ordered. Someone who orders a steak should carry more of the service charge than someone who only had a salad, and most groups find this the fairest way to split.

Example: Sam orders a NT$600 steak, Jo orders NT$300 pasta, and they share NT$200 of fries. The restaurant adds a 10% service charge, which is common in Taiwan.

The bill comes to NT$1,210. If Sam pays at the counter, Jo sends Sam NT$440 and you are done. If it is Jo's birthday and you mark Jo as treated, Jo's NT$440 is shared by everyone else who is paying.

Bill splitting FAQ

What is the fairest way to split a service charge?

Split it in proportion to what each person ordered: each person's service charge = what they ordered × the service charge rate. People who order more pay more, and nobody who ordered lightly ends up subsidizing the others. The itemized mode does this automatically.

Should we split evenly or pay for what we ordered?

If everyone ordered roughly the same, splitting the total evenly is quickest. If the prices are very different, such as a steak next to a salad, paying for what you ordered is fairer. You can switch between the two methods in one tap to compare.

How do shared dishes work?

Pick several people under “Who ordered it” and the dish is split between them. For a dish the whole table shared, tap “Whole table” and it is split between everyone.

How do we treat someone, for example on their birthday?

Mark them under “Who doesn't pay”. Their share, including what they ordered and their part of the service charge, is divided between everyone else who is paying. Everyone else's own items are still counted as usual.

Can it read the items on the receipt?

Yes. First copy the receipt text with your phone's text recognition: on iPhone, press and hold the text in the photo or tap Live Text; on Android, use the Text option in Google Lens. Then tap “Paste receipt text” in the tool. Item names, quantities and prices become items, and the service charge and total are filled in too, so all that is left is tapping who ordered what.

Is my receipt photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The photo and any pasted text are processed only on your own phone. They are not uploaded and are not included in share links, and the photo is gone once you close the page.

Can my friends edit the bill from the shared link?

No. Only the person who started the bill can edit it. Friends who open the link can see what everyone ordered and owes, but they cannot change it, and it does not replace any bill saved on their own phone. If you switch phones, use “Copy your organizer link” to keep editing on the new one.

How do I keep track of who has paid me back?

First choose who paid under “Who paid the bill?”. When a friend sends you their share, tap “Got it” next to their name in the results. The screen shows how many people have paid and how much is still outstanding, and when you tap “Share with the group” or “Copy summary”, the message marks who has already paid, so you don't chase people twice.

How is this different from the trip expense splitter?

Use this tool for one bill paid by one person, where the point is splitting item by item and sharing the service charge. For a trip with many expenses paid by different people, often in foreign currencies, use the trip expense splitter instead.

Add the bill splitter to your home screen for next time

Once added, it opens like an app from your home screen — nothing to download, no storage used. Open it when the bill arrives.

iPhone: add to Home Screen (Safari)

  1. Open this page in Safari
  2. Tap the Share icon next to the address bar (a square with an up arrow; on newer iOS, tap “⋯” first)
  3. Scroll down and tap “Add to Home Screen”
  4. Rename it if you like, then tap “Add”

iPhone: add to Favorites

  1. Tap the Share icon again
  2. Tap “Add to Favorites” — it will show up on Safari's start page

iPhone: add to Home Screen (Chrome)

  1. Open this page in Chrome
  2. Tap the Share icon on the right of the address bar (or tap “⋯” at the bottom right, then “Share”)
  3. Scroll down and tap “Add to Home Screen”
  4. Tap “Add” — the icon appears on your home screen

iPhone: add a Chrome bookmark

  1. Tap “⋯” at the bottom right
  2. Tap “Add to Bookmarks” (shown as a ☆ in some versions)

“Add to Home Screen” in Chrome needs iOS 16.4 or later. If you don't see it, do it once in Safari.

Android: add to home screen (Chrome)

  1. Open this page in Chrome
  2. Tap the “⋮” menu at the top right
  3. Tap “Add to home screen”, then “Add” or “Create shortcut”

Android: bookmark it

  1. Tap “⋮” at the top right
  2. Tap the ☆ star in the top row

Samsung Internet: tap “≡” at the bottom → “Add page to” → “Home screen”.

Computer: bookmark it

  1. Press Ctrl+D (Windows) or ⌘+D (Mac)
  2. Save it to your bookmarks bar for one-click access
For security, websites can't add themselves for you — the steps above take about 10 seconds.

How do I copy text from a receipt photo?

iPhone: open the receipt in Photos, tap the Live Text icon at the bottom right (or press and hold the text) → Select All → Copy.

Android: open the receipt in Google Photos, tap Lens → Text → Select all → Copy text.

You can also point your camera at the receipt and tap the text frame when it appears.
