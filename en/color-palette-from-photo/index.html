    How to make a color palette from a photo

    1. Pick a photo with colors you like: A film still, a travel snap, an illustration or a product shot all work. Tap Choose a photo, drag the file into the box, or paste it with Ctrl/⌘+V.
    2. Set the number of colors: Slide between 3 and 12 colors. Leave Keep small bright colors on if a small accent, like a red sign, should make it into the palette. Sort light to dark, by how much each color is used, or by hue.
    3. Drag the dots to fine-tune: Each dot on the photo is one color. Drag it and the swatch changes to the color under it, with a magnifier so your finger doesn’t hide the spot. Add up to 30 colors with + Add a color, and use Undo if you change your mind.
    4. Take it where you work: On iPad, Send to Procreate opens the share sheet; pick Procreate. For web and UI work, switch to HEX, RGB or HSL and copy the codes or ready-made CSS variables into Figma, Canva or your stylesheet. Download a square, 4:5 or story-size card to share the palette.

    Tips for a better palette

    • Film and TV stills work well: a production designer already balanced the colors for you.
    • Try the same photo at different sizes: 5 colors suit a logo or slide deck, 8 to 10 suit an illustration.
    • For a website, take one light color for the background, one dark color for text and one bright color for buttons. The White text / Black text tags tell you which pairs are readable.
    • Dark or color-cast photos give dull palettes. Brighten the photo first, then upload it.

    FAQ

    Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

    No. The colors are read inside your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, and the photo is gone when you close the page.

    How do I get the palette into Procreate?

    On iPad, tap Send to Procreate and choose Procreate in the share sheet. The palette appears at the bottom of Colors → Palettes. If Procreate isn’t in the list, the .swatches file downloads instead; open it from Downloads in the Files app. A Procreate palette holds up to 30 colors.

    Can I use the colors in Figma or CSS?

    Yes. Pick HEX, RGB or HSL, then tap Copy all codes for a plain list or Copy CSS variables for a :root block you can paste into a stylesheet. In Figma, paste a HEX code into the fill color field.

    Can it tell me the Pantone number?

    No. Pantone numbers are licensed data, and a photo’s colors shift with light, camera and screen, so they can’t be matched to an exact print color. You get the on-screen (sRGB) codes; check a physical Pantone guide under standard lighting if you need a Pantone match.

    Why doesn’t a color look exactly like the photo?

    Each swatch is a real pixel picked as the most typical of its group, not an average, so colors stay clean. If a color you want is missing, raise the number of colors or drag a dot onto that spot.

    What do White text and Black text mean?

    They show the contrast ratio of white or black text on that color. 4.5 or higher meets WCAG AA, so normal-size text stays readable. Use it when choosing background colors for a site or slides.

    Can I use it with CLIP STUDIO PAINT or Photoshop?

    Not as a file yet. For now, copy the HEX codes and add them by hand. Export to CLIP STUDIO (.cls) and Photoshop (.aco) is planned for the app version.
