How to make a phone wallpaper in five steps

  1. Check your phone model: open this page on your phone and it uses your screen resolution. On a computer, pick the phone you want the wallpaper for.
  2. Pick a background: 12 soft, muted colors such as Sakura, Sage and Sky. Want your own? Open “Custom background”.
  3. Pick a flower and its color: wildflower, carnation, magnolia, daisy, lavender, baby's breath, rose, ping pong mum, sunflower, or a mix. Let the color match automatically or choose one.
  4. Pick an arrangement: a single stem, taped to a card, tied with a ribbon, wrapped, potted, a branch, or text only. Tap “Rearrange” for a different layout.
  5. Add a few words and save: the words are yours to write (a name, a date, any short line), or tap one of the phrases, choose a font, add a flowing line or a little heart, then tap “Save to phone”.

Tips for a soft, simple wallpaper

Short words look best. Two to four words fit nicely in handwriting. The 15 built-in phrases are all short and kind, from “stay gold” to “you are doing great”.

Keep the home screen calm. App icons sit on top of it, so a single stem or a small pot works well. Save the fuller bouquets for the lock screen.

The bottom is the safest place for words. The clock and widgets take the top, and notifications can cover the middle.

Phone wallpaper maker FAQ

Can I use and share the wallpapers I make?

Yes. The flowers, tape, ribbons, wrapping paper and pots are original illustrations drawn in code by Knitting Hiyori. Use them as wallpapers, send them to friends or post them online. Please don't sell the images themselves.

Where do the handwriting fonts come from?

All six come from Google Fonts. Sacramento, Allura and Caveat (English), LXGW WenKai TC (Chinese) and Klee One (Japanese) use the SIL Open Font License; Homemade Apple uses the Apache 2.0 license. Both are open licenses, free to use, including commercially.

What size should I choose?

Just pick your phone model. The tool exports a PNG at your screen's resolution, for example 1179 × 2556 for iPhone 16. If your model isn't listed, pick the closest size; your phone scales it when you set the wallpaper.

I tapped “Save to phone” but nothing downloaded.

On a phone, a share sheet opens. Choose “Save Image”. If your browser can't share files, the tool shows the image instead: press and hold it, then tap “Add to Photos” or “Download image”.

Can I write in Chinese or Japanese?

Yes. Chinese uses the handwritten-style LXGW WenKai TC, and lines with hiragana or katakana use the Japanese handwriting font Klee One, sized to match the English handwriting. The four English fonts above only change English letters.

Will the clock cover my words?

Turn on the clock preview to see roughly where the clock and the bottom buttons sit. It never appears in the saved image. “Below flowers” is the safest choice.

Can I make it on my computer?

Yes. Pick your phone model, click “Save to phone” to download the PNG, and send it to your phone. Or share the settings link with yourself and open it on your phone.

Is anything uploaded?

No. The wallpaper is drawn in your browser. Nothing is uploaded; the tool only remembers your last settings on this device.

Can I suggest more flowers or features?

Please do. Email ideas for flowers, layouts, fonts or anything else to newsletters@knittinghiyori.com. The ping pong mum and sunflower were added this way.

How can a friend make the same wallpaper?

Tap “Share these settings with a friend”. The link opens with the same background, flowers, arrangement and words, sized for their own phone.

Add the wallpaper maker to your home screen

Once added, it opens like an app from your home screen. Nothing to download, no storage used. Open it whenever you want a fresh look.

iPhone: add to Home Screen (Safari)

  1. Open this page in Safari
  2. Tap the Share icon next to the address bar (a square with an up arrow; on newer iOS, tap “⋯” first)
  3. Scroll down and tap “Add to Home Screen”
  4. Rename it if you like, then tap “Add”

iPhone: add to Favorites

  1. Tap the Share icon again
  2. Tap “Add to Favorites” — it will show up on Safari's start page

iPhone: add to Home Screen (Chrome)

  1. Open this page in Chrome
  2. Tap the Share icon on the right of the address bar (or tap “⋯” at the bottom right, then “Share”)
  3. Scroll down and tap “Add to Home Screen”
  4. Tap “Add” — the icon appears on your home screen

iPhone: add a Chrome bookmark

  1. Tap “⋯” at the bottom right
  2. Tap “Add to Bookmarks” (shown as a ☆ in some versions)

“Add to Home Screen” in Chrome needs iOS 16.4 or later. If you don't see it, do it once in Safari.

Android: add to home screen (Chrome)

  1. Open this page in Chrome
  2. Tap the “⋮” menu at the top right
  3. Tap “Add to home screen”, then “Add” or “Create shortcut”

Android: bookmark it

  1. Tap “⋮” at the top right
  2. Tap the ☆ star in the top row

Samsung Internet: tap “≡” at the bottom → “Add page to” → “Home screen”.

Computer: bookmark it

  1. Press Ctrl+D (Windows) or ⌘+D (Mac)
  2. Save it to your bookmarks bar for one-click access
For security, websites can't add themselves for you — the steps above take about 10 seconds.
